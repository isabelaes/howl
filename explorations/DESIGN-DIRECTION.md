# Howl design direction

**Chosen starting point:** Round 02, version B — *More readable* (19px post text), building on Round 01, direction B — *Redline*. This direction is based on the saved exploration; no refined Figma frame was provided.

## The feel

Howl should feel loud, warm and quick: a candid stream of campus life, easy to read at a glance. Let confident type and direct student language supply the energy. Keep the interface informal and human, never like a bank app.

## Color

Keep Redline’s palette: true black `#000000` for the page and feed; soft white `#F5F5F5` for primary text; gray `#A4A4A4` for handles and quiet actions; charcoal `#303030` for dividers; and Howl red `#CC0000` for the Howl mark and primary actions. Preserve these roles and colors rather than adding decorative hues or gradients.

## Type

Use Arial for headings and body text. Keep usernames small and bold, handles and locations quiet, and post copy at the selected 19px with comfortable line spacing. Let the words lead; keep labels and controls secondary.

## Shapes and layout

Use a narrow, centered feed on laptop with navigation on the left and restrained supporting content on the right. On phones, give posts the full feed width with comfortable edge spacing. Separate posts with thin gray rules rather than cards. Avatars can be small circles; controls are plain text or simple shapes. Red buttons use a modest corner radius and clear, generous tap targets.

## Do

1. Keep the selected black, white, gray and Howl-red color roles consistent.
2. Keep post text at the chosen readable size and let real student posts take priority.
3. Give each post a clear author line, message and quiet action row.
4. Use thin dividers and open space to distinguish posts without enclosing them in cards.
5. Make the phone feed comfortable to read and the laptop layout deliberately centered.

## Don’t

1. Don’t introduce purple, gradients, glow, glass effects or decorative color.
2. Don’t shrink post copy back to the hard-to-read size from the earlier Redline direction.
3. Don’t use campus illustrations, generic financial-app styling or decorative card stacks.
4. Don’t make quiet metadata or actions compete with the post itself.
5. Don’t stretch the phone layout across a laptop or let controls cover the feed.
